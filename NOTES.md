## 1945 migrations from Hungary
About 25,000 Poles returned from Hungary in 1945, then only 834 in 1946 and a few dozen a year after that (26,119 for 1945-49 on the map). They were mostly wartime refugees of September 1939 (Hungary sheltered tens of thousands of soldiers and civilians) plus old economic emigrants from the late 19th century, mostly workers around Budapest, who are called "reemigrants". Poland had no embassy in Budapest, and the Soviet-influenced Allied Control Commission delayed restoring relations. Lt. Col. Leszek Krzemień was appointed government delegate on 29 May 1945, and a repatriation committee ran from 12 July to 15 September 1945, funded with 15 million pengő from the Hungarian government. Historians put the number of Poles in Hungary at 50,000 to 140,000, so the 25,000 may be only part of them.
Source: [Sylburska 2021, Prace Historyczne 148(2)](https://ejournals.eu/pliki_artykulu_czasopisma/pelny_tekst/4fde003f-8047-46b7-923a-9bba94481226/pobierz), quoting Kersten 1974. Certainty: low-medium; one secondary source, with the 1945 figure rounded.

## Greek refugees
Greek Civil War refugees (Greeks and Macedonians) came to Poland from autumn 1948 after the defeat of the communist side, as part of an evacuation to the Eastern Bloc (about 55,000 people, 28,000 children, across the bloc). The first two rail transports in September-October 1948 brought 1,013 children (513 Greek, 500 Macedonian), another 2,092 came in April 1949, and the first adult group reached Zgorzelec on 16 December 1949, with more in 1950. Wounded partisans were treated in a secret military hospital at Dziwnów (operation "Szpital", 1949-50, about 1,500 patients by November 1949). They were settled in Lower Silesia, Western Pomerania and the Lubusz region, mostly in state farms, and most were rural people (96% of agricultural origin in 1950). The total is 12,000 to 14,000 (Pudło 1995: 12,000; Wojecki 1989: 14,000; IPN 2016: more than 13,000); another source says about 20,000, probably counting those born in Poland. The refugee population fell from about 11,000 in 1957 to 7,700 in 1975, and most returned to Greece in 1975-1990 after the junta fell, with about 5,500 using the 1982 decree. This probably explains the GUS peak of registered emigration to Greece in 1986-87 (710 and 911), which is an inference.
Sources: [Nowicka 2017, Balcanica Posnaniensia 24](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/bp/article/download/6857/12159/24585); [Sienkiewicz 2022](https://econjournals.sgh.waw.pl/KSzPP/article/download/2855/2727/6039); [Wojecki 2000, Studia Zachodnie 5](https://zbc.uz.zgora.pl/Content/45737/download); [IPN 2016](https://ipn.gov.pl/pl/wydawnictwo/do-pobrania/publikacje-obcojezyczne/233032,PRL-a-wojna-domowa-w-Grecji-The-Polish-Peoples-Republic-and-the-Greek-Civil-War.pdf); [Wudalas on the Dziwnów hospital](https://repository-edulll.ekt.gr/edulll/retrieve/11248/3443_1.5.1.%20Wudalas.pdf). Certainty: medium for the children's counts and the overall range, low for the split between 1949 and 1950-54 and for the return explanation.
