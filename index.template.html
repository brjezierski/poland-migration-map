Migration to and from Poland, 1945 to 2024

Each line links Poland to another country for a five-year period; it is drawn as a bundle of thin lines, and the more people moved the more lines it has. Teal lines run to Poland, orange lines out of it. Before 1990 the figures are rough hand-made estimates. After that, departures use official arrival counts where they exist (most of Europe, the US and Australia) and otherwise estimates from UN migrant-stock changes plus assumed deaths; the sources table below the map says which for every country. For arrivals to Poland from 2010 you can switch to Poland's own residence-permit data.

Sources and methods by country

For the country chosen here (or clicked on the map), one row per period: the figures drawn, where they come from, how they were derived and how far to trust them. Neighbouring periods that share a source and method are merged, and a reason is given for every low confidence. The to-Poland rows follow the "Arrivals to Poland" choice above the map. Flows of 1,000 people or fewer are listed but not drawn.

How to read it

  • The number of lines in a bundle grows with the square root of the number of people, on one scale for every period, so small flows still show; the key on the map shows reference bundles. Dashed lines are hand-curated estimates. The numbers in the side panel are exact.
  • "Arrivals to Poland" chooses what the teal lines show from 2010: growth in the UN count of foreign-born residents (default), or first residence permits issued to non-EU citizens. Permits are summed over the years of each period (2020–2024 covers five calendar years) and are not arrivals. The temporary-protection options use the number of people registered under it in Poland at the end of December 2024 (almost all Ukrainians), which is a stock, not arrivals; the last option adds it to all permits and can count a person twice. They largely leave out refugees: Ukrainian permits fall in 2022 while about a million Ukrainians arrived, which suggests those arrivals are not counted in them. EU citizens need no permit either. Departures from Poland use official immigration counts into the receiving country where they exist (European countries, mainly 1998–2024; these are gross counts and larger than net stock growth) and otherwise the stock measure with assumed deaths added.
  • Click a country (or a name in the list) to see its flows in every period. Scroll or use the buttons to zoom, drag to pan. Escape clears the selection, the arrow keys step through periods.
  • 1945–1989: hand-curated estimates, rounded, each with a source and confidence in the sources table below the map. Only flows I could attach a figure to are shown, so many real movements are missing.
  • 1990–2024: growth in the number of foreign-born people in Poland (to Poland) and of Poland-born people abroad (from Poland) between UN reference years. Falling stocks are shown as zero.

Limits

  • For countries without official counts these are net changes in stocks plus assumed deaths, not counts of moves. Deaths, returns, naturalisation and revised estimates hide gross flows, so inflows in particular are understated until the 2020s.
  • Stocks count people by country of birth, so ethnic Germans born in Poland, or Poles born in the former eastern territories, appear as migrants.
  • Internal resettlement such as Operation Vistula (1947) is not migration to or from Poland and is left out. So is movement between countries other than Poland.
  • The last period, 2020–2024, is four years long and includes the arrival of Ukrainians after February 2022.
  • Only flows above 100 people are drawn for migration counts and estimates, and above 1,000 for permits and temporary protection, and for the stock measure only the 30 largest partners per direction.
  • Before 1990 the map draws eastern Europe with the borders of the time: the Soviet Union, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia as single states, and East and West Germany apart. They are selected, listed and counted separately from Russia, Czechia, Slovakia, Serbia and the other successor states, whose figures start in 1990. Yugoslavia has no figures yet. The inner German border is hand-placed on a coarse outline, so it is approximate. Other borders that changed (for example Austria's occupation zones) are not shown.
