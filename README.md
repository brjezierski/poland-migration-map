# Poland migration map

An interactive world map with a timeline from 1945 to 2024. For each five-year period, static arcs link Poland to other countries, each drawn as a bundle of thin lines: the more people moved, the more lines. Teal lines run to Poland, orange lines out of it, and both directions are drawn at once for every partner country. Dashed lines are hand-curated estimates.

Open `index.html` directly in a browser. It is self-contained and needs no network access.

## Using the page
- Play, or drag the slider, or press the left and right arrow keys, to move through the periods.
- The number of lines in a bundle grows with the square root of the number of people, on one scale for all periods; the key on the map shows reference bundles. The numbers in the side panel are exact.
- Teal lines (to Poland) show the default measure only: growth in the UN count of foreign-born residents, which includes refugees, replaced by official counts where the sources table says so. Departures use official immigration counts into the receiving country where they exist, otherwise the same stock measure. The alternative arrival measures (first residence permits, temporary protection, their sum) and the yearly permits panel are kept on the branch `testing-arrivals-methodologies`.
- Click a country (or a name in the list) to see its flows in every period, with a small history chart. Escape clears the selection.
- Scroll or use + and − to zoom, drag to pan, ⌂ for the whole world, EU for a close-up of Europe.

- "Sources and methods by country" (below the map) has a table for any country, chosen in its selector or by clicking the map: one row per period with the figures, source, methodology, and confidence with the reason for every low one. Neighbouring periods that share a source and method are merged.

## Files
- `data/map.json`: simplified country outlines (generated by `tools/prepare_map.py`; Crimea is drawn as part of Ukraine).
- `data/historical_1945_1989.json`: hand-curated flows, each row with a note, source and confidence.
- `data/flows.json`: all flows per period (generated by `tools/prepare_flows.py`). Every value carries a basis (source, methodology, confidence and, for low confidence, a reason; stored once in `bases`). `build.py` fails if a drawn value has no basis or a low confidence has no reason. Each curated row in `data/historical_1945_1989.json` needs a `method` and, when its confidence is low, a `why`.
- `index.template.html`: the page. `build.py` inlines the data and writes `index.html` (generated, do not edit by hand). Run `python3 -I build.py` after changing the template or the data. It fails if a flow points at a country with no map shape.

Rebuild everything:

`tools/prepare_flows.py` needs openpyxl (`requirements.txt`). Python here is externally managed, so use a project venv: `python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt`, then run the commands below with `.venv/bin/python -I` in place of `python3 -I`.

```
python3 -I tools/prepare_map.py --src ne_110m_admin_0_countries.geojson
python3 -I tools/prepare_flows.py --cache /tmp/poland-cache
python3 -I build.py
```

The flows script downloads the UN workbook into `--cache`, checks the column layout and prints a summary per period. The Natural Earth GeoJSON is `geojson/ne_110m_admin_0_countries.geojson` from <https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector>.

## Data
- **1990 to 2024:** UN DESA, International Migrant Stock 2024, Table 1 (stock by destination and origin, both sexes; reference years 1990, 1995 ... 2020, 2024), POP/DB/MIG/Stock/Rev.2, CC BY 3.0 IGO. For each partner country, the growth of the stock of its people in Poland is drawn as flow to Poland, and the growth of the stock of Poland-born people living there as flow from Poland. Shrinking stocks count as zero. The last period, 2020 to 2024, is four years long. Migration counts and estimates of 100 people or fewer, and permit and protection figures of 1,000 or fewer, are not drawn on the map, and for the stock measure only the 30 largest partners per direction are kept.
- **1945 to 1989:** no systematic bilateral data exists, so the rows in `historical_1945_1989.json` are rounded figures from the literature named in each row: the post-war population transfers (Germans leaving, Poles repatriated from the USSR, Ukrainians resettled), German Aussiedler statistics, and US immigration yearbooks, plus the Jewish emigration to Israel after 1956. **These figures were entered from memory of the published numbers and were not re-checked against the sources while building this page. Treat them as order of magnitude and verify before quoting.** US figures are decade totals split evenly across two periods. The exception is Israel: those rows come from the Israeli Central Bureau of Statistics (Statistical Abstract of Israel 2010, Table 4.4, immigrants by period of immigration and country of birth: 106,414 for 1948-51, 39,618 for 1952-60, 14,706 for 1961-71, 6,218 for 1972-79, 2,807 for 1980-89). Those periods do not match the five-year grid, so they are split evenly by year, except 1952-60, which is placed entirely in 1955-59. The table counts people born in Poland, and the real year-by-year pattern (for example the 1968-69 peak) is not visible. Australia 1945-54 is derived from census counts of Poland-born residents (about 6,600 in 1947, about 56,600 in 1954), whose growth of about 50,000 is split evenly by year; it is a net stock change and is flagged low confidence. Later Australian census counts barely move, because deaths offset arrivals, so Australia is modelled instead for 1955-2004: the change in the Poland-born stock (ABS table 5.37 census counts to 1981, UN DESA estimates from 1990) plus deaths, spread evenly by year. The death rates (0.3% a year in the 1950s rising to 1.9% by 2001-05) were calibrated against real data: the ABS series below imply 2.1-2.6% a year for 2005-2020 (net migration minus the change in the stock, divided by the stock), about half of what my first guess assumed, so I halved the earlier rates too. These modelled rows are net of emigration and return, are flagged low confidence and depend on rates I assumed, not on data. From 2005 Australia uses real counts: ABS Overseas Migration 2022-23, tables 2.1, 3.1 and 1.1 (`data/australia_abs.json`), Poland-born overseas migrant arrivals, departures and net overseas migration by financial year to 2022-23. Arrivals (about 1,200-1,400 a year, 340 in 2020-21) are drawn; they are people who stay 12 of the next 16 months, include temporary migrants and returning residents, and did not necessarily arrive from Poland. Departures (about 600-950 a year) are in the panel note. 2020-24 has only three financial years. New Zealand 1945-54 is about 744 Polish displaced persons (1949-51, from Kresy-Siberia and Te Ara census counts), split by year; it is under the 1,000-person drawing cutoff, so it shows only in the panel. Only flows with a figure I could attach are included, so many real movements, such as the emigration around 1968 and 1981, appear only in notes.

Where Eurostat has official immigration with Poland as the previous residence (`migr_imm5prv`, partner PL; 36 European countries, mostly 1998-2024, stored as `from_pl` in `data/yearly.json`), a period uses the sum of its calendar years. Up to two missing years inside a period are interpolated, and if one end year is missing the other four are scaled by 5/4; both are flagged in the row's note. These counts are gross registrations or survey estimates, so they are larger than net stock growth and are defined differently by country (registration rules, minimum stay). Germany and the UK show the largest rises when a period switches from the model to the official count.
  2. Everywhere else, and for periods the official series does not cover, the net inflow is modelled as the change in the UN Poland-born stock plus assumed deaths. "Settled" diasporas (a 1990 stock of 20,000 or more, plus Canada and New Zealand) use the death rates calibrated on the Australian data (1.4% a year rising to 2.9%); all other countries, mostly recent arrivals, split the stock into an old cohort dying at 4.5% a year and young arrivals at 0.4%. Modelled values below 1,000 are dropped. These rows are low confidence, net of emigration and return, and depend on rates I assumed.
  3. Before 1990 only the countries with their own sources (Germany, the Soviet Union, Israel, the US, Australia, New Zealand and a few others) have rows; for other countries no pre-1990 stock series was found.
  The earlier rule, which kept only positive UN stock growth for the 30 largest partners, is gone, so a country whose stock fell can now show a positive modelled inflow.
- **Map:** Natural Earth 110m countries (public domain), Natural Earth projection, simplified with shapely. For periods before 1990 (1945-49 to 1985-89) the page swaps in the borders of the time for eastern Europe, built in `tools/prepare_map.py` (`old` in `data/map.json`): the Soviet Union (15 republics merged), Czechoslovakia (Czechia and Slovakia), Yugoslavia (Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia, Serbia, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Kosovo), and Germany split into East and West along a hand-placed approximation of the inner German border (no state-level data at 110m, so the line is coarse and West Berlin is not shown). Poland's current borders are the post-1945 borders, so they are unchanged. The side-panel graph of a former state (Soviet Union, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia) ends with the 1985-90 period, and hovering any graph shows the figures of the period under the cursor. Clicking an old shape selects the modern country the flows are filed under (Soviet Union to Russia, either Germany to Germany).

- **`data/yearly.json`:** only `from_pl` (immigration into other European countries with Poland as the country of previous residence, Eurostat `migr_imm5prv`, partner PL), built by `python3 -I tools/prepare_yearly.py --cache <dir>`. The yearly totals, permits and temporary-protection series and the panel that drew them are on the branch `testing-arrivals-methodologies`.

## Limits
- From 1990 the values are net changes in stocks, not counts of moves. Deaths, returns, naturalisation and revised estimates hide gross flows, so inflows to Poland are understated until the 2020s.
- Stocks are by country of birth, so ethnic Germans born in Poland or Poles born in the former eastern territories appear as migrants. The UN's 1990 to 2000 stocks are partly modelled.
- Internal resettlement (Operation Vistula, 1947) and movement between other countries are not shown. The Soviet Union is drawn on Russia.
- A five-year bin hides timing: the 2022 Ukrainian arrivals fall inside 2020 to 2024.
- Five micro-states in the UN data (Malta, Faroe Islands, Liechtenstein, Bermuda, San Marino) have no shape on the 110m map and are skipped.
- I checked the page in light mode and at phone width; dark mode is styled but was not visually checked.
